How our predictions work

The CTA's own arrival estimate — the "Scheduled" time — comes straight from the CTA Train Tracker. Our "Predicted" time layers a machine-learning model on top of it.

The model (gradient-boosted trees) is trained on months of historical arrivals we collect every few minutes. For each train approaching a station it learns how the CTA's estimate typically drifts, using features like the line, station, direction, time of day, day of week, whether it's rush hour, how quickly the ETA has been changing, and the gap to the trains ahead and behind. From those it predicts how many minutes early or late the train will really be versus the CTA estimate, and we show you the corrected time.

It retrains every week on fresh data, so it keeps adapting. Predictions are strongest on the Red and Blue lines (where we have the most data) and improve on the other lines as we collect more. Your accuracy feedback (the +/− control on each train) feeds back into this.
